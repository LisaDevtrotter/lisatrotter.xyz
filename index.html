recent rabbit holes

one tab leads to twenty more. each card shows the trail and the page i stopped on. open a trail and follow it yourself.

hole 017sep 2026

a receipt for nothing, and gold in the seine

  1. yves klein
  2. immaterial zones
  3. the receipt ritual
  4. a 2017 remake
  5. burn addresses

23 tabs · 1 h 40

ended at an address ending in dEaD

the earliest thing i’d call an nft came with no image, and the full version of the sale ended in a fire. i’d like to know how klein’s buyers felt, walking home with empty hands.

follow the trail
  1. yves klein a painter famous for one shade of blue. this story has no paint in it.
  2. zones of immaterial pictorial sensibility 1959. you paid klein in gold, and he wrote you a receipt for an empty zone.
  3. the ritual. if you wanted the complete work, you burned the receipt and klein threw half the gold into the seine.
  4. 2017. mitchell f. chan rebuilds the idea on ethereum, receipts included.
  5. burn addresses. you send tokens to an address with no known key, and they stay there. klein ran the same idea on a river, sixty years earlier.

hole 016sep 2026

a million pixels at a dollar each

  1. milliondollarhomepage
  2. pixel ads
  3. dead links
  4. r/place
  5. basepaint

31 tabs · 2 h 05

ended on today’s basepaint canvas, with the brush open and no plan.

the buyers kept their squares, and a lot of them let the domain behind the square expire. i clicked along one row and stopped counting the dead links halfway.

follow the trail
  1. the million dollar homepage 2005. alex tew, a student in england, sells a million pixels at a dollar each to pay for university.
  2. the story in january 2006 tew auctioned the last thousand pixels on ebay. you can visit the page today, frozen at that moment.
  3. dead links. click around and you land on expired domains, parked pages, and the odd shop that made it.
  4. r/place, 2017. for three days, each reddit user could place one pixel, then wait five to twenty minutes. you get flags, and strangers negotiating treaties over border feuds.
  5. basepaint a new shared canvas each day. strangers paint it together, then collectors mint the result as one piece.

hole 015sep 2026

algorithms without a computer

  1. vera molnár
  2. machine imaginaire
  3. 1968
  4. georg nees, 1965
  5. art blocks

18 tabs · 1 h 10

ended rereading (des)ordres and measuring how far each square drifts from straight.

she wrote algorithms for nine years before a computer ran one of them. i think of her when a client asks for a framework before we’ve talked about the problem.

follow the trail
  1. vera molnár born in hungary, based in paris from 1947, one of the first artists to make work with computers.
  2. machine imaginaire. from 1959 she ran algorithms by hand, following simple rules on paper, step by step.
  3. 1968. she gets access to a real computer. by then she has nine years of rules ready to run.
  4. georg nees stuttgart, february 1965, one of the first shows of computer drawings. some of the artists who came did not accept it as art.
  5. art blocks the artist writes a script, and your mint runs it to make a new output. molnár’s method, plus a wallet.

hole 014sep 2026

websites that skipped the redesign

  1. berkshirehathaway.com
  2. space jam, 1996
  3. info.cern.ch
  4. view source
  5. oldweb.today

27 tabs · 1 h 25

ended on info.cern.ch, clicking through the first website’s plain blue links.

the oldest pages i opened loaded fastest. the next morning i went back to a client’s homepage and deleted two scripts.

follow the trail
  1. berkshire hathaway one of the largest companies in the world, with a homepage made of plain links in a list. it loads before your finger leaves the trackpad.
  2. space jam, 1996 the studio kept the original movie site online. tables, gifs, a starfield.
  3. info.cern.ch cern restored the first website in 2013. its subject is the web itself.
  4. view source. a lot of people learned html this way: open someone else’s page and read it. i commented this one for that reason.
  5. oldweb.today you open old sites in emulated old browsers. netscape navigator feels like a place you visit.

hole 013aug 2026

art that runs as long as the chain runs

  1. autoglyphs
  2. code in the contract
  3. terraforms
  4. hic et nunc goes dark
  5. teia

35 tabs · 2 h 40

ended asking each “onchain” piece one question: where does the image live?

most “onchain art” stores a link to a file on someone’s server. owning one of the few that don’t feels different, in a way i haven’t pinned down yet.

follow the trail
  1. autoglyphs 2019. larva labs put the drawing code inside the contract, so each of the 512 glyphs comes out of ethereum itself.
  2. code in the contract. you call a function and get the glyph back as plain text characters, ready to paste anywhere.
  3. terraforms a land-art piece by mathcastles. the contract builds each parcel from chain state, character by character.
  4. hic et nunc, november 2021. the founder takes the site down. the contracts keep running underneath.
  5. teia the community puts a new front end on the same contracts within days. collectors find their pieces where they left them.

hole 012aug 2026

a globe full of radios

  1. radio.garden
  2. a faroese talk show
  3. numbers stations
  4. uvb-76
  5. the conet project

22 tabs · 3 h 05

ended at 3 am, listening to a buzz.

strangers log a buzzing frequency with dates and times, some of them for years, with no answer in sight. i admire that patience, and i know i don’t have it.

follow the trail
  1. radio.garden spin the globe, land on a live station. i landed in the faroe islands.
  2. a faroese talk show. i understood none of it and stayed twenty minutes. the host laughed a lot, which helped.
  3. priyom.org volunteers log numbers stations here: voices reading digits over shortwave, with no government willing to explain them.
  4. uvb-76 a station on 4625 khz that has sent a short buzz for decades. now and then a voice reads out a few russian words.
  5. the conet project irdial-discs, 1997. four cds of numbers station recordings, free to stream on the internet archive.

hole 011aug 2026

please be patient, this page is under construction

  1. under construction gifs
  2. geocities, 1994
  3. the 2009 shutdown
  4. archive team
  5. one terabyte of kilobyte age

26 tabs · 2 h 15

ended on a tumblr bot that publishes a rescued geocities page every twenty minutes.

thousands of strangers apologised, in blinking gifs, for pages they had not finished. i have a staging server full of pages that deserve the same gif.

follow the trail
  1. under construction hundreds of “under construction” gifs saved from geocities. you can read the old neighbourhood in most of the file names.
  2. geocities founded in 1994. from 1995 you could claim a free homepage in a themed neighbourhood, with a street number of your own.
  3. october 2009. yahoo, which bought geocities in 1999, closes the us service. the japanese version lasts until 2019.
  4. archive team volunteers download as much as they can before the deadline. a year after the shutdown they release it as one torrent, 641 gb compressed.
  5. one terabyte of kilobyte age olia lialina and dragan espenschied dig through the torrent and publish screenshots of the pages, each one shown in a browser from its year.

    04 / depth 640 m
    
onchain, without the prices

    
i leave the charts to other people. these are the ideas i keep returning to, each with the part i can’t settle yet.

  


  

    

      
art2019

      
autoglyphs

      
interesting because you ask the contract and it returns the image as text. to see a glyph, you need ethereum and a text box.

      
not sure yet i suspect people in fifty years will look at thumbnails and skip the code.

    

    

      
culture2021

      
nouns

      
interesting because one auction each day, with no end date. the treasury pays for strange public things, glasses and short films among them.

      
not sure yet i’d like to see the ritual in year ten, once the novelty has worn off.

    

    

      
socialprotocol

      
farcaster

      
interesting because your account id sits in an onchain registry, and a shared network stores your posts and follows. you can switch apps and keep both.

      
not sure yet i’d like numbers on how many people switch apps once they can. my guess is few, as long as their friends stay put.

    

    

      
identityens

      
names you own

      
interesting because one name can point to a wallet and a website. you hold it the way you hold a token.

      
not sure yet i haven’t decided how i feel about an identity you can lose by forgetting a renewal.

    

    

      
wallets0x…dEaD

      
burn addresses

      
interesting because you can watch the balance grow on a block explorer, and no one holds a key to spend it.

      
not sure yet i can’t decide if a burn is a statement or accounting with better branding.

    

    

      
bitcoin2009

      
the genesis block

      
interesting because it quotes a headline from the times of 3 january 2009, and its first 50 coins can’t be spent. people keep sending it small amounts anyway.

      
not sure yet i can’t tell if tipping an address that has never spent a coin is devotion or a guestbook with fees.

    

    

      
artbase

      
basepaint

      
interesting because whoever shows up paints the day’s canvas, collectors mint it as one piece, and the painters get a share.

      
not sure yet i’m curious what happens to the pictures once painters start optimising for the payout.

    

    

      
archive2023

      
inscriptions

      
interesting because casey rodarmor’s ordinals gave images in bitcoin blocks a standard format and a number each. you can pull a jpeg out of the same ledger that records payments.

      
not sure yet every node that keeps the full history stores those images for good. i haven’t seen a fair answer on who should carry that cost.

    06 / depth 960 m
    
about

  


  

    
    

      
i’m lisa, from france. i build websites for clients during the day.

      
devtrotter is dev plus globe-trotter, though most of the trotting happens between tabs.

      
i’m curious about crypto, digital art and the stranger corners of the internet. i read the fine print, and i tend to have one more question.

      
client work means i check a site’s load time before i look at the design. the old sites on this page pass that test with ease.

      
with crypto, i read the contracts and the design docs and leave the price talk to others. my standing question for a new project: who changes their behaviour once real money is on the line?

      
i keep my finds here and add to the page when a tab survives a full week. if you know a strange corner i haven’t seen, send me the link.

      
colophon · i wrote the html, css and a little javascript by hand. the page sets no cookies. when it loads, i log the visit: time, page, country, region and city, network, browser family, language, where you came from, how long the page stayed open, how far you scrolled and which sections you saw. no ip addresses. every night i delete entries older than 90 days, and cloudflare, which hosts the site, keeps database backups for up to 30 days. fonts come from this server: bricolage grotesque, instrument serif, space mono, silkscreen. the small bubbles are canvas, and the big one is a webgl shader.
